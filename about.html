About

I’m Parth Mittal — computer science at Cornell University, class of 2028, from Torrance, California.

Aerospace is the pull; software architecture is the craft. The problems I chase are the ones where a system has to be designed, not just written — a spacecraft deciding what to compute onboard versus what to send down, a drone holding a pose estimate together without GPS, a simulated world replaying a million ticks onto the same bit-identical state. Getting the layering, the contracts, and the failure modes right is the part I would do for free.

At Cornell I work in two research groups — space-instrument software in Dr. Savransky’s SIOSLab and motion analysis in Dr. Ferrari’s LISC — and I’m a co-author on two papers currently under review. Along the way, internships have taken me through GPS-denied drone navigation and compiler-level fuzzing infrastructure, and I led networks for a US Navy radio project through MIT Lincoln Laboratory’s SOCOM Ignite program.

Everything here ships under The Private Panda — the name my work has carried since before any of it was worth reading. Away from a keyboard: mountain biking, cycling, and reading.
